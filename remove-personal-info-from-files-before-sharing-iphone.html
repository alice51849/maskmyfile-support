How to remove personal info from a PDF, photo or file before you share it on iPhone

A landlord wants your pay stubs, a client needs a copy of a file, an office asks for an ID copy, or support wants a screenshot. You want to send the document, not everything printed on it. To remove info safely, take the private values out of a new copy, not just cover them, and check that copy before it leaves your phone. Here is what to remove, how to do it on an iPhone, and how to confirm it is really gone.

What personal info to remove

DocumentInfo to removeUsually fine to keep
Rental application or pay stubBank account numbers and payment details the landlord did not ask forYour name, employer, pay period and totals the landlord asked for
ID copyNumbers and barcodes the recipient does not needName and photo, if the recipient must confirm who you are
Client fileOther clients' names, emails, phone numbers and addressesThe parts of the file the recipient is working on
ScreenshotPhone numbers, emails, faces and account details in the backgroundThe part of the screen you meant to show

A simple rule: remove anything the recipient does not need to do their job. Keep the rest so the document still makes sense.

Covering info is not removing it

A black box drawn on top of a PDF can leave the text underneath, so it can still be selected, copied or searched. A cropped screenshot or photo can still carry metadata. Real removal means the private value is gone from the new file itself.

Check the copy before you send it

  1. Open the new copy, not the original.
  2. Search it for one value you removed, such as the last digits of an account number or an email domain. On a PDF, also try selecting text where the redaction sits.
  3. Look over the whole page or image once more for info in headers, footers, signatures or the background.

Mask My File reopens and verifies the new copy for you, but a ten-second look of your own is still a good habit.
